Mahjong ABCs & 123s

This is the no-assumed-knowledge version: what the tiles are, what the words mean, and what actually happens during a game.

The Three Numbered Suits

Dots, also called circles, are numbered 1 through 9.

Bam tiles, short for bamboo, are numbered 1 through 9.

Crak tiles, short for characters, are numbered 1 through 9.

Winds, Dragons, Flowers & Jokers

Winds are East, South, West, and North. They are not numbered suits.

Dragons are red, green, and white. The white dragon is often called “soap.”

Flowers are special tiles. Jokers may substitute in certain groups, but not singles or pairs.

How groups are named

Pair, Pung, Kong & Quint

Pair

Two identical tiles.

Pung

Three identical tiles.

Kong

Four identical tiles.

Quint

Five identical tiles. A joker is normally needed because only four natural copies exist.

A game from start to finish

Six Simple Steps

01

Learn The Tile Families

First separate Dot, Bam, and Crak tiles from winds, dragons, flowers, and jokers. You do not need to memorize winning hands yet.

02

Sort Your Rack

Place each suit in number order. Keep pairs and repeated numbers together so possible directions are easier to see.

03

Use The Card Like A Recipe

The current American Mahjong card lists the exact combinations that can win. Pick a line your rack is naturally moving toward instead of forcing one immediately.

04

Pass During The Charleston

Before normal play, players pass groups of three unwanted tiles according to the table’s Charleston sequence. Protect useful pairs and connected groups.

05

Draw One, Discard One

On a normal turn, draw a tile, decide whether it helps, and discard one tile. Watch what other players discard and expose.

06

Match One Line Exactly

A winning hand contains 14 tiles arranged exactly like one line on the card being used. When complete, call “Mahjong.”

Common words at the table

Mahjong Terms In Plain English

Rack

The holder that keeps your tiles standing and organized.

Wall

The stacked rows of face-down tiles players draw from.

Charleston

The opening exchange in which players pass groups of three tiles before normal turns begin.

Exposure

A completed group placed face-up after calling another player’s discard.

Concealed

A hand played without calling discards for exposures, except when allowed to claim the winning tile.

Joker Swap

Replacing a joker in an exposed group with the exact natural tile it represents.

Call

Taking another player’s discard because it completes a legal exposed group or wins the hand.

Mahjong

The declaration made when your tiles exactly complete a winning hand on the card being used.

Pattern Recognition & Strategy

Skip the basic vocabulary and work directly with suit structure, repeated numbers, honors, flowers, difficulty, and defensive judgment.

Stay Flexible Longer

Compare at least two plausible card lines until the Charleston and early discards clarify the stronger route.

Read Availability

Track discards and exposures before committing to a tile that may already be dead or dangerous.

Protect Joker Value

Use jokers where they preserve the most structure, and watch exposed groups for possible swaps.

Shift To Defense

When your hand stalls, stop feeding likely hands and prioritize safe discards over wishful drawing.

Before you buy

Mahjong Shopping FAQ

What does a beginner need to start playing?

A practical starter setup includes an American Mahjong tile set, four racks with pushers, a current card for each player, and a mat or protected playing surface.

Should I buy full-size or travel tiles?

Choose full-size tiles for regular home play and a more substantial feel. Travel tiles are thinner and lighter when portability and storage matter more.

Do I really need a Mahjong mat?

A mat is not mandatory, but it protects the table, softens the sound of the tiles, and makes shuffling and dealing more comfortable.

Are the pattern-trainer examples official hands?

No. They are original visual exercises designed to demonstrate tile relationships without reproducing official card content.
